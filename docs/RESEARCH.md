# 1. Research and experiment journey

1. **Start with the measurement problem.** Grammar scoring from speech involves both transcription and rating. ASR can normalize errors, omit repairs, or hallucinate text; the same transcript can conceal timing and acoustic information. My first practical question was whether a cheap transcript model could establish useful signal before training larger models.

2. **Build an interpretable baseline.** Whisper Small transcribed all 985 recordings. Word/character TF-IDF and Ridge regression gave six-fold pooled OOF RMSE 1.11795, compared with 1.24253 for the training-fold mean reference. Its accepted public score was 0.6607. Fold-specific vocabulary/IDF prevented fitting the text representation on validation examples.

3. **Test the ASR assumption.** I changed only the transcript input to Whisper Large-v3 while holding the scoring configuration and folds fixed. Words changed in 909 recordings, and corpus word disagreement against Small was 20.09%; this is disagreement between models, not human-reference WER. Pooled local RMSE worsened to 1.12624 on five of six folds, while public RMSE improved to 0.6522. The approximate paired-record bootstrap interval for the local RMSE difference included zero. Larger ASR was not a consistent downstream improvement.

4. **Investigate verbatim text.** CrisperWhisper offered another view of fillers, repairs, and repeated speech. I kept transcription failure cases visible, retained all labels, and avoided grammar correction as a preprocessing step. Licensing and model-access requirements remain those of the upstream checkpoints; weights and generated transcripts are not redistributed here.

5. **Audit sources, zeros, and joins.** The sample submission had 204 unrelated IDs, while the actual test CSV listed 216 recordings. Exports therefore followed the real test manifest. Source analysis identified related clean/noisy recordings and unusual zero cases. Filenames can overlap between train and test, so feature joins use both split and filename. These checks mattered before any architecture comparison.

6. **Move from text to multimodal representations.** Contextual DeBERTa text representations were combined with intermediate/final speech-encoder summaries. A subsequent spectrogram branch retained information omitted by global vector pooling. The code supports CNN alternatives and pretrained AST. The audited AST model improved pooled OOF RMSE from the earlier CNN fusion's approximately 0.51592 to 0.49524; audio hidden width also changed from 512 to 2,048, so this does not isolate AST's causal contribution.

7. **Use error research to decide what to try next.** The audit examined error concentration, training curves, prediction calibration, label slices, transcript disagreement, duration, quietness, padding, and source relationships. It did not justify relabelling difficult recordings or applying blanket score expansion. Lower AST learning rates, controlled audio-width comparisons, native AST frontend checks, and temporal-feature experiments were proposed as separate controls; they must not be presented as completed improvements.

8. **Compare ensemble families.** Frozen OOF predictions allowed relatively cheap tests of Elastic Net, robust regression, convex blends, polynomial/Ridge corrections, multiscale kernels, SVR, tree models, and evolutionary combinations. Greater flexibility frequently overfit inner selection or failed to improve the outer development screen. The broad later screen did not establish a substantial development improvement. Model count alone was not a reliable guide.

9. **Treat the hurdle result as a caution as well as a score.** Learned zero/nonzero gating improved the recorded public trajectory when blended with a retained joint ensemble. However, the 60% version had slightly worse development RMSE than the 35% version despite better public RMSE. This is evidence that tiny reused-development differences did not reliably rank public outcomes. Neither outcome proves generalization, particularly when unusual zeros and public-informed selection are involved.

10. **Research provenance.** This narrative reconstructs saved code, experiment memory, metadata, and archived results.
