# 1. Research foundations and references

1. **Why use intermediate transformer layers?** My feature design combined layers 4, 8, and 12 rather than assuming the final layer was the best universal representation. Layer-wise speech research studies how acoustic, phonetic, and word-level information is distributed through transformer depth. This provides a rationale for retaining intermediate features and comparing their downstream usefulness; it does not prove that one middle layer always wins, or that my chosen channels outperform a final-only control on SHL. Relevant primary papers are [Layer-wise Analysis of a Self-supervised Speech Representation Model — Pasad, Chou, and Livescu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04734) and [Comparative layer-wise analysis of self-supervised speech models — Pasad, Shi, and Livescu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03929). The precise arXiv paper originally recalled during this project has not been uniquely identified; these are directly relevant references, not an invented exact attribution.

2. **How that idea appears in the code.** WavLM contributes layer-4 mean/std, layer-8 mean/std, and layer-12 mean; HuBERT and wav2vec2 each contribute layer-4 mean plus layer-12 mean/std. Mean pooling summarizes channel level and standard deviation captures temporal variation, while discarding much ordering. The resulting 8,448-dimensional vector feeds a learned MLP. These depth choices are documented in [the settings](../configs/ast_original_settings.py), [extraction code](../experiments/audio_channels/channel_runtime.py), and [architecture account](ARCHITECTURE.md). A final-layer-only ablation remains necessary to isolate the value of the intermediate channels.

3. **A prior competition example I examined.** The 11 October experiment memo records inspection of pasted inference code from **Feedback Prize — English Language Learning**. It used five DeBERTa-v3-large configurations, including mean pooling, weighted-layer pooling, and LSTM processing of layer representations, with fold prediction averaging. The SHL adaptation requires one target rather than six essay traits. This informed the exploration of contextual representations and pooling diversity. The original attachment's public URL is not retained, so no specific Kaggle author or notebook is attributed here. Its layer-wise LSTM mechanism should also not be confused with the implemented SHL token-level bidirectional LSTM pooler.

4. **Contextual text representations.** [DeBERTaV3: Improving DeBERTa using ELECTRA-Style Pre-Training with Gradient-Disentangled Embedding Sharing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09543) is the backbone reference for the main text encoder. This work uses its contextual features for supervised regression; it does not use an instruction prompt to ask DeBERTa for a rating. [Microsoft's implementation](https://github.com/microsoft/DeBERTa) documents the model family.

5. **Self-supervised speech backbones.** [WavLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.13900), [HuBERT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07447), and [wav2vec 2.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11477) provide the pretrained speech representations used in the feature branch. Their original objectives and downstream benchmarks differ from this grammar assessment. The project tests transfer of those representations rather than claiming the pretrained encoders directly measure grammatical proficiency.

6. **Speech proficiency as related work.** [Proficiency assessment of L2 spoken English using wav2vec 2.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13168) and the [self-supervised pronunciation-scoring implementation](https://github.com/hy310/ssl_finetuning) appear in the saved backbone research notes. They support investigating speech representations for assessment, not treating pronunciation, overall proficiency, and grammar as identical targets.

7. **Spectrogram transformers.** [AST: Audio Spectrogram Transformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01778), [the original implementation](https://github.com/YuanGongND/ast), and [the Transformers AST documentation](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/model_doc/audio-spectrogram-transformer) are recorded in the AST error research. They ground the spectrogram branch and the proposed frontend/LR controls. The project's saved HTK frontend differs from the native setup; the adaptation is disclosed rather than assumed equivalent.

8. **ASR failure modes.** [Careless Whisper: Speech-to-Text Hallucination Harms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08021) is recorded in the transcript-error audit. It motivated checking disagreement and silence-associated failures. It does not establish that a particular competition transcript is hallucinated or that disagreement is human-reference WER.

9. **Stacking and validation.** The saved ensemble research cites [StackingRegressor](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.ensemble.StackingRegressor.html), [nested validation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/auto_examples/model_selection/plot_nested_cross_validation_iris.html), and [GroupKFold](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.model_selection.GroupKFold.html). These explain the use of cross-fitted predictions and grouped selection, and why prefit/frozen base models do not give a fully nested end-to-end estimate. [Kernel ridge regression](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.kernel_ridge.KernelRidge.html) and [SciPy differential evolution](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.optimize.differential_evolution.html) ground the residual-kernel and evolutionary screens. Optimizer sophistication is not evidence of better generalization.

10. **Imbalanced regression: investigated, not adopted as a reported improvement.** [Delving into Deep Imbalanced Regression](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/yang21m.html) and [Balanced MSE for Imbalanced Visual Regression](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Ren_Balanced_MSE_for_Imbalanced_Visual_Regression_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf) are cited in the error-research record as potential weighting/loss controls. The audited model still trains with original-unit MSE and is selected by unweighted RMSE. This repository does not claim to have implemented or validated those papers' methods.

11. **Attribution scope.** References documented in working notes are identified above. Backbone papers provide implementation background, and the layer-wise papers provide directly relevant support for the user's recalled research motivation. No unrecorded lecture attendance, exact prior reading date, or copied competition solution is claimed. Research informs hypotheses; controlled experiments establish whether they help this task.
