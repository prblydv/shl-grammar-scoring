# 1. Validation, overfitting, and generalization

1. **The aim is generalization.** A lower public score is useful feedback, but it is not sufficient evidence that a system will assess new speakers, source batches, or recording conditions reliably. The controls below reduce particular risks; they do not establish that overfitting has been eliminated.

2. **Keep related sources together.** Data investigation identified clean/noisy source relationships. The grouped neural protocol kept 31 verified pairs together using score-bin group stratification. Later analysis supported 35 of 37 candidate relationships, including three newly found pairs that crossed the frozen assignment. Those historical folds were not silently rewritten: the existing scores retain that limitation. A stronger future comparison would rebuild groups and retrain every baseline under the revised protocol. Unknown speaker overlap may also remain.

3. **Fit preprocessing only on training partitions.** The baseline fits TF-IDF vocabulary and IDF separately within each fold. The multimodal pipeline fits audio imputation/normalization and mel statistics on training folds. Meta-learning fits its scalers, feature transforms, classifiers, and regressors within the corresponding meta-training partitions. Validation rows do not supply their own normalization or supervised target fit.

4. **Use OOF predictions instead of fitted training predictions.** A base model predicts each OOF row from a fold model that excluded that row. Ensembles learn from these frozen OOF columns, with held-out meta-folds and grouped inner parameter selection. Train/test namespaces are aligned independently; labels, groups, folds, and reported RMSE are checked. Identical OOF exports of the same run count once. This avoids treating a CV export and a full-data export as two independent models.

5. **Control capacity and training duration.** Early stopping selects the best validation-RMSE checkpoint rather than the last epoch. AdamW decay, fusion dropout, and regularized meta-models constrain fitting. Full-data refits use a fixed epoch count derived from CV, not an invented full-data validation score. The AST model nevertheless has roughly 623 million trainable parameters for a small training set; these controls do not remove its generalization gap.

6. **Avoid brittle rules.** Filename order, source blocks, low loudness, ASR failures, and transcript similarity were investigated as diagnostics. They are not defensible substitutes for grammar assessment. The neural model has no hand-written rule assigning zero from a filename or source lookup, and its original labels were not replaced. Preserving disfluencies also prevents preprocessing from making poor grammar look artificially fluent.

7. **Why the zero-case experiment is questionable.** The 37 zero-labelled recordings show unusual source/noise patterns. A learner can distinguish those examples without necessarily learning a transferable grammar criterion. A later experimental hurdle ensemble learned `P(score > 0 | base predictions)` and multiplied it by a positive-score regressor. Its inputs are model predictions, not filenames or held-out labels; nevertheless, statistical learning can still exploit a dataset discrepancy. Replacing a hand-written shortcut with a learned gate does not prove the shortcut has disappeared.

8. **What the scores support.** The hurdle-containing submission achieved the lowest recorded public RMSE, 0.3141. It is retained for transparency, not endorsed as the best-generalizing solution. The earlier non-hurdle quadratic/kernel ensemble scored 0.3166 publicly. We do not have an independent deployment-like evaluation that ranks those systems. The public improvement cannot be attributed to particular hidden examples without access to their labels.

9. **Remaining selection risks.** Base OOF predictions are frozen rather than regenerated in every outer meta-fold, so the full stack is not fully nested. Repeated model-family/recipe search reuses development data. Test columns mix full-data, CV-ensemble, and best-fold inference distributions. The final 35%, 45%, and 60% hurdle weights were explored after public feedback. All of these can introduce optimism or public-leaderboard overfitting.

10. **The stronger test I would want.** Freeze the complete recipe, rebuild all known source groups, and evaluate on genuinely unseen source groups/speakers with a fully nested comparison where feasible. Report both overall RMSE and zero/positive slices, plus a positive-only comparison that removes the unusual-zero advantage. Compare the non-hurdle pipeline and hurdle experiment under that same untouched protocol. Until then, private performance and out-of-distribution generalization remain unknown.
